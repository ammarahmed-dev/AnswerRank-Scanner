Open the llms.txt of a few software companies and you will see the same shape every time: a title, a one-line summary and a list of links. It looks trivial, and it is. This guide explains what the file is for, what it cannot do, and how to write one that is worth publishing. It is part of our [technical AEO guide](/blog/technical-aeo-guide).

## What llms.txt is

`llms.txt` is a plain Markdown file at the root of your domain. It was proposed at [llmstxt.org](https://llmstxt.org) as a way to hand language models a clean, curated overview of a site. Web pages are full of navigation, scripts and banners. A model that wants to understand your business quickly has to dig through all of it. llms.txt skips that and says: this is who we are, and these are the pages that matter.

It is the sibling of two files you already know:

- `robots.txt` controls which crawlers may fetch which URLs.
- `sitemap.xml` lists URLs for search engines to discover.
- `llms.txt` summarizes the site in readable prose and links to the best pages.

## What llms.txt does not do

Be honest about expectations before you spend time on it. Major AI search companies have not publicly confirmed that they use llms.txt as a ranking or citation signal, and a file alone will not get you cited. What it gives you is a small, cheap, authoritative description in your own words, which some developer tools may read. If a human or an agent wants a quick map of your site, it is useful there.

That makes it a good low-effort addition once the basics are in place: [AI crawlers allowed in robots.txt](/blog/robots-txt-ai-crawlers), content that can be read without JavaScript, clear titles and schema. It is not a substitute for any of them.

## The format

The proposal defines a simple structure, in this order:

1. An H1 with the name of the site or project (the only required part).
2. A blockquote with a short summary.
3. Optional paragraphs with extra context.
4. H2 sections that contain lists of links, each written as `- [Title](url): short description`.
5. An optional section named `Optional` for secondary pages that can be skipped when a shorter context is needed.

## A copy-paste example

```md
# Acme Invoices

> Acme Invoices is invoicing software for freelancers. It creates invoices, tracks payments and sends reminders.

## Product

- [Features](https://www.example.com/features): What the invoicing app does
- [Pricing](https://www.example.com/pricing): Plans and limits

## Docs

- [Getting started](https://www.example.com/docs/start): Send your first invoice in five minutes
- [API reference](https://www.example.com/docs/api): REST endpoints and authentication

## Blog

- [How to invoice international clients](https://www.example.com/blog/international-invoicing): Taxes, currency and payment terms

## Optional

- [Privacy policy](https://www.example.com/privacy)
- [Terms of service](https://www.example.com/terms)
```

## How to create yours

1. **Write the summary first.** One or two sentences: what you do and who it is for. If you cannot say it plainly, your homepage probably cannot either.
2. **Pick 10 to 30 pages.** Product, pricing, documentation, your best guides, and the company page. Skip tag archives, search results and thin pages.
3. **Describe each link in a few words.** Say what the reader gets from the page, not just the title.
4. **Move legal and utility pages to `Optional`.** Privacy, terms, login and checkout pages are rarely what a model needs.
5. **Publish at the root.** Save the file as `llms.txt` so it loads at `https://yourdomain.com/llms.txt` with a 200 status. In Next.js, put it in `public/`.
6. **Keep it current.** Update it when you launch or remove an important page.

Do not want to write it by hand? The free [llms.txt generator](/tools/llms-txt-generator) reads your homepage and sitemap, groups the pages into sections and gives you a draft to edit.

## Common mistakes

**Dumping the whole sitemap.** The value is curation. A list of 2,000 URLs is just a worse sitemap.

**Linking to pages that block crawlers.** If a linked page is disallowed in robots.txt or sits behind a login, the link is no use to anyone. Check your [AI crawler access](/tools/ai-crawler-checker) too.

**Marketing copy in the summary.** "The world's leading solution" tells a model nothing. State what the product is and does.

**Wrong location or status.** A file in `/docs/llms.txt`, a redirect to the homepage or a soft 404 that returns HTML will not count as a real llms.txt.

## How common is it?

In our [study of 85 well-known sites](/research/ai-readiness-study), 36 publish an llms.txt file: 32 of 49 software companies and none of the 21 media sites. Adoption is concentrated in software and developer documentation, which fits what the file is best at: giving tools a map of a docs-heavy site.
