## Why Your Website Might Be Invisible to ChatGPT and Perplexity

Google visibility and AI search visibility are not the same thing. You can rank in Google's top three results and still be completely absent from ChatGPT answers. Understanding why requires knowing how AI answer engines select their sources.

AI answer engines do not crawl and rank billions of pages the way Google does. They pull from a curated set of sources they consider accurate, authoritative, and machine-readable. Pages that make it into that set share a few common traits: clear structured data, well-organized content, open crawl access for AI bots, and links from trusted sources.

The four most common reasons a website is invisible in AI search:

**1. No structured data.** Without JSON-LD schema markup, AI engines cannot tell what type of content your page contains. They have to guess - and they often skip pages where they have to guess.

**2. Thin or poorly structured content.** AI engines extract answers from web pages. If your content does not clearly answer questions in a logical, scannable format, AI systems pass you over for a page that does.

**3. Blocked AI crawlers.** A significant number of sites accidentally block GPTBot, PerplexityBot, or ClaudeBot through `robots.txt` rules, security plugins, or CDN configurations. Blocked crawlers cannot index your content at all.

**4. No external citations.** Authority matters to AI engines just as it does to Google. If no recognized source links to or mentions your site, AI engines treat it as unverified and deprioritize it.

### Method 2 - Ask ChatGPT Directly

You can ask ChatGPT whether it knows about your brand or domain. This reveals whether your site is in ChatGPT's training data, though it comes with real limitations worth understanding.

Open [chat.openai.com](https://chat.openai.com) and try these prompts:

- `"Do you have any information about [your brand name]?"`
- `"What do you know about [yourdomain.com]? Has it been mentioned in content you were trained on?"`
- `"When someone asks about [your product category], what websites or tools do you recommend?"`

Look at the results critically. If ChatGPT says it has no information about your brand, or returns clearly wrong information, that is a data gap. If competitors appear in the category recommendations and you do not, that is your AEO gap made visible.

**The limitations of this method:** ChatGPT's training data has a knowledge cutoff date. Even if you have done significant AEO work recently, ChatGPT may not yet reflect it. Results are also inconsistent - running the same prompt twice can return different sources on different days. Use this as a rough indicator, not a definitive audit.

### Method 3 - Check Perplexity AI Directly

Perplexity differs from ChatGPT in one critical way: it pulls from the live web, not a static training dataset. That makes it a better real-time signal of your current AI search visibility.

Go to [perplexity.ai](https://perplexity.ai) and run queries your target customers would actually ask:

- `"Best [your product category] tools in 2026"`
- `"How do I [describe the main problem you solve]"`
- `"[Your topic] guide for beginners"`

Perplexity shows its sources in a numbered panel alongside every answer. Check whether your domain appears. Sources listed higher tend to shape the answer more heavily than those at the bottom.

Since Perplexity uses live web data, improvements you make to your structured data, content structure, and crawler access can show up within days to weeks - far faster than waiting for a ChatGPT training data update.

## How to Fix Poor AI Search Visibility (7 Actionable Steps)

If your visibility check revealed gaps, this is the repair sequence to follow.

**Step 1 - Add FAQ schema to key pages.**
Any page that answers more than two questions is a candidate for `FAQPage` structured data. AI engines specifically parse FAQ schema to extract answer candidates. Add a JSON-LD `FAQPage` block to your most important landing pages, product pages, and blog posts. For most sites, this is the single highest-returning AEO improvement.

**Step 2 - Write direct, answer-first content.**
AI engines reward content that gets to the point fast. Each section of your page should open with the core answer in the first one to two sentences, then elaborate. Rewrite your three most important pages to follow this pattern. Long windup paragraphs that bury the answer are extremely common - and they cost you citations.

**Step 3 - Add Organization and Article schema.**
Your homepage needs an `Organization` JSON-LD block with your name, URL, and a one-sentence description. Every blog post and editorial page needs a `BlogPosting` or `Article` block with `headline`, `datePublished`, `author`, and `publisher` filled in. These tell AI engines exactly what they are reading before they parse a single word of body text.

**Step 4 - Create an `llms.txt` file.**
This is an emerging AI-specific standard, similar in purpose to `robots.txt` but for context rather than crawl access. A plain text file at `yourdomain.com/llms.txt` gives AI systems a concise summary of what your site is, who you are, and how your content is organized. Not every AI engine reads it yet, but it signals AEO awareness and is increasingly recognized by Perplexity, Claude, and others.

**Step 5 - Get cited on authoritative sources.**
AI engines trust sites that other trusted sites already reference. Practical ways to build those citations: respond to HARO or Qwoted requests in your niche, write guest posts on established industry publications, and ensure you are listed in reputable software directories relevant to your category. Even a handful of citations from recognized domains can meaningfully shift how AI engines treat your content.

**Step 6 - Fix technical blockers.**
Open your `robots.txt` file right now. If you see `GPTBot`, `PerplexityBot`, `ClaudeBot`, or `Google-Extended` in a `Disallow` rule, remove those lines and redeploy. Also review any Cloudflare WAF rules, WordPress security plugins, or CDN bot-filtering settings that may be blocking these crawlers from reaching your pages. This is often a one-line fix with immediate impact - and it is the first thing an AEO checker will flag if it is present.

**Step 7 - Monitor your AI visibility over time.**
AEO is not a one-time fix. AI engine behavior evolves, new competitors optimize, and your own site changes in ways that can introduce regressions. Set up ongoing [AI visibility monitoring](/monitor) to track your score across time, get alerted when key signals drop, and measure your progress after each round of optimizations. Visibility you build today can erode next quarter without active tracking.

## ChatGPT Visibility vs Google Ranking: Key Differences

It is tempting to treat AEO as an extension of SEO, but the mechanics are different enough to change your priorities:

| | Google SEO | AI Search (AEO) |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Rank in a list of links | Get cited in a direct answer |
| Core signals | Backlinks, keywords, technical health | Schema, content structure, crawler access, authority |
| Output | Position 1–10 on a results page | Mentioned or quoted in an AI-generated response |
| Update speed | Weeks to months | Days to weeks (live tools) / months (training data) |
| Measurement | Search Console, rank trackers | AEO score, direct AI testing, citation monitoring |

The practical implication: strong SEO and strong AEO reinforce each other, but you cannot rely on Google rankings to predict AI visibility. Research consistently shows that the majority of sites cited in ChatGPT answers are not the top Google results for the same query. The two systems weight different signals.
