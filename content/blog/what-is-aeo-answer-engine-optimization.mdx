**Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)** is the practice of optimizing your website so that AI-powered tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews cite it when generating answers. Where SEO focuses on ranking in a list of links, AEO focuses on being the answer itself.

If you are searching for what is AEO, the short answer is this: AEO (answer engine optimization) helps your website get cited in AI search results from tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI results.

## What Is AEO?

**Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)** is the practice of structuring your content and website so that AI-powered search tools select it as a cited source when generating answers to user questions.

Unlike traditional SEO, which asks "where does my page rank?", AEO asks "does my brand get mentioned when someone asks a relevant question?"

AI answer engines include:
- **ChatGPT** (800+ million weekly users as of early 2026)
- **Perplexity** (growing fast as a research-first AI)
- **Google AI Overviews** (appearing in over 50% of all Google searches)
- **Microsoft Copilot** (powered by Bing's index)
- **Google Gemini**

These tools generate direct answers. They do not show ten links - they show one synthesized response with a small number of cited sources. Being one of those sources is the new SEO.

---

## AEO vs SEO

They are not opposites. AEO builds on the same foundation as SEO. But they optimize for different outcomes.

| | SEO | AEO |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Rank in search results | Be cited in AI answers |
| Measures | Rankings, clicks, traffic | Mentions, citations, AI referrals |
| Content focus | Keywords and backlinks | Structure, authority, answer-readiness |
| Primary platforms | Google, Bing | ChatGPT, Perplexity, AI Overviews |

The good news: most AEO best practices also improve SEO. Well-structured, authoritative content ranks better in Google and gets cited more often by AI engines. You do not have to choose between them.

## How Answer Engines Choose What to Cite

Understanding the process helps you optimize for it. AI answer engines use a method called Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):

**Stage 1 - Query interpretation**
The AI parses what the user is really asking - not keyword matching, but understanding intent, entities, and context.

**Stage 2 - Retrieval**
The system searches its index for pages semantically relevant to the query. It pulls candidate pages based on conceptual similarity, not just keyword overlap.

**Stage 3 - Ranking and selection**
Retrieved pages are scored on relevance, authority, recency, and structural quality. Only the highest-scoring pages make it into the final answer.

**Stage 4 - Answer generation**
The AI synthesizes a response using the selected sources and cites them.

Your job is to be in the candidate set at Stage 2, and then score highly enough at Stage 3 to make it into the final answer.

## What AI-Cited Content Looks Like vs What Gets Ignored

The difference between a page that earns a citation and one that gets skipped comes down to answer-readiness - not word count or domain authority alone.

**A page AI engines skip:**
A 3,000-word article titled "Everything About Content Marketing in 2026" with a single H1, dense unbroken paragraphs, no schema markup, and a generic meta description. When someone asks ChatGPT "what is content marketing?", the AI cannot extract a clean, attributable answer from this page. It skips to a competitor whose page answers the question directly in the first sentence under a clear heading.

**A page AI engines cite:**
A 1,200-word page titled "What is Content Marketing? Definition and Examples" that opens with a two-sentence definition under an H2 framed as the same question. It has `FAQPage` schema, `Article` JSON-LD, a meta description matching the topic, and a `robots.txt` that allows GPTBot and PerplexityBot. The AI extracts the clean answer, attributes it to the source, and cites it.

The structural change is not adding 2,000 more words. It is restructuring content so the answer comes first, the source is clearly labeled, and the crawl path is open.

## The 6 Core AEO Signals

These are the signals that most directly determine whether your content makes it into an AI-generated answer.

### 1. Structured data
JSON-LD schema markup tells AI engines exactly what your page is - an article, a product, a FAQ, an organization. Pages with schema are cited significantly more often than pages without it. Priority types: `Organization` on your homepage, `Article` or `BlogPosting` on editorial pages, `FAQPage` on any page that answers multiple questions.

### 2. Direct answers
AI engines extract 40-75 word chunks from pages to use as answers. Each section should open with a direct, self-contained answer to the question implied by its heading. Write for extraction: the answer first, context second. Burying the answer three paragraphs into a section costs you citations.

### 3. Authority and citations
Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness signals tell AI engines they can trust your content. This includes named authorship, About and Contact pages, publication dates, and citations from third-party sources. A study found 85% of AI brand mentions originate from third-party sources - external validation matters as much as on-page quality.

### 4. Crawlability
AI crawlers must be able to access your site. Many sites accidentally block GPTBot, PerplexityBot, or ClaudeBot in their `robots.txt` file - making them completely invisible to AI search, regardless of content quality. Fix this first.

### 5. Content clarity
AI engines extract chunks from pages. Content written in long unbroken paragraphs is harder to cite accurately. Use question-format H2/H3 headings, short clear paragraphs, and FAQ sections on relevant pages. The goal is modular content that reads clearly out of context - a paragraph that makes sense on its own is a paragraph that can be cited.

### 6. Entity recognition
AI engines understand the web in terms of named entities - brands, products, concepts, and their relationships. When an AI engine clearly knows who you are and what you do, it is more likely to include you as a relevant source. This means: consistent use of your brand name across your site, an `Organization` schema block with your `name`, `url`, and a clear `description`, and content that explicitly defines the entities your site covers rather than using vague or interchangeable terminology.

**Performance note:** Core Web Vitals matter too. Slow pages get deprioritized by AI engines and Google AI Overviews. Target LCP under 2.5 seconds, CLS under 0.1, and TBT under 200ms.

## How AEO Differs Across Platforms

Each AI engine has slightly different behavior when selecting sources:

**ChatGPT Search** - heavily influenced by Bing's web index. Strong Bing rankings = better ChatGPT visibility. Named authorship and E-E-A-T signals matter a lot.

**Perplexity** - crawls the web in near real-time. Freshness matters more here than ChatGPT. Rewards content with clear question-format headings and frequently updated pages.

**Google AI Overviews** - pulls mostly from Google's existing top-10 results. Traditional SEO still applies, but content needs to be structured for snippet extraction.

**Microsoft Copilot** - relies heavily on Bing's index. Particularly useful for B2B queries. LinkedIn presence matters for brand entity recognition.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What does AEO stand for?**

AEO stands for Answer Engine Optimization - the practice of optimizing web content so it gets cited and summarized by AI-powered answer engines like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews.

**Is AEO the same as SEO?**

No. SEO optimizes for ranking in traditional search results. AEO optimizes for being cited in AI-generated answers. They complement each other - strong SEO signals help AEO - but AEO requires additional signals like structured data and direct question-and-answer content that SEO alone does not cover.

**What is the difference between AEO and GEO?**

GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is another term for the same concept. AEO tends to focus on answer engines specifically, while GEO is broader and includes all generative AI interfaces. The terms are often used interchangeably.

**How do I know if my site is AEO optimized?**

Run a [free AEO scan](/scan) on AEOCheck - it checks your site against 20+ AI visibility signals and returns a score with specific issues to fix.

**What is the minimum viable AEO optimization?**

The highest-impact starting points are: adding FAQ schema to key pages, restructuring content so H2s are questions and the first paragraph answers them directly, and adding Organization structured data to your homepage.

**How long does AEO take to show results?**

For Perplexity, structural fixes like robots.txt and schema can show results within 2-6 weeks. Broader AI visibility across ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews typically takes 2-4 months of consistent effort.

**Do I need a big website to benefit from AEO?**

No. AI engines prioritize relevance and answer quality over brand size or domain authority. A small site with well-structured, authoritative content on a specific niche topic can outperform much larger sites in AI citations.
