**Start with accurate product information, appropriate schema and accessible pages. Then measure actual ChatGPT answers.** There is no documented markup recipe or citation count that guarantees your product a recommendation. A crawler check or an AEO score can reveal website issues; it cannot establish that an AI answer included your brand.

This guide separates three tasks: describing the right product, making its facts available, and observing whether it is recommended. For a practical starting point, [check your product-page markup](/tools/schema-checker) or [view an example readiness report](/sample-report).

## What schema helps ChatGPT understand your products?

Choose markup that describes the page you actually publish. The vocabulary tells systems what your data means; it does not reveal the ranking formula of a recommendation engine.

| Page or entity | Appropriate vocabulary | Facts to verify before publishing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An individual product | [Product](https://schema.org/Product) | Name, description, image, brand, identifiers and canonical product URL |
| A purchasable offer | Offer, associated with the product | Actual price, currency, availability and destination for that offer |
| A software application | [SoftwareApplication](https://schema.org/SoftwareApplication) | Name, application category, supported operating system and applicable offers |
| The business behind the page | [Organization](https://schema.org/Organization) | Business name, website and genuine identity references |
| Visible questions and answers | FAQPage | The questions and answers users can read; this is not an inclusion requirement |

Use product identifiers only when they exist for the item. Do not turn a quote-only service into a free product with a fictional zero-price offer. A software page and a retail product page may need different properties and different validation rules.

### Worked example: one product and its seller

The following is an **original, fictional example**, not a customer result or a live offer. Imagine a page whose visible content says: Cedarfield sells the white 800 lm Adjustable Desk Lamp, SKU LAMP-WHITE-800, for USD 49.00 and it is in stock. Its description and image match that exact variant. The markup below records those same facts and connects the offer's seller to the organization by `@id`.

```html
<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@graph": [
    {
      "@type": "Organization",
      "@id": "https://example.com/#organization",
      "name": "Cedarfield",
      "url": "https://example.com/"
    },
    {
      "@type": "Product",
      "@id": "https://example.com/products/lamp-white#product",
      "name": "Adjustable Desk Lamp, White, 800 lm",
      "url": "https://example.com/products/lamp-white",
      "description": "White desk lamp with an adjustable head and 800 lm light output.",
      "image": "https://example.com/images/lamp-white.jpg",
      "sku": "LAMP-WHITE-800",
      "brand": { "@type": "Brand", "name": "Cedarfield" },
      "offers": {
        "@type": "Offer",
        "url": "https://example.com/products/lamp-white",
        "price": "49.00",
        "priceCurrency": "USD",
        "availability": "https://schema.org/InStock",
        "seller": { "@id": "https://example.com/#organization" }
      }
    }
  ]
}
</script>
```

Replace every example value with verified page facts before using it. Keep inventory and price synchronized with the visible page. Do not add invented reviews, ratings, identifiers or a made-up expiry date to satisfy a tool. An `@id` connects declarations; it is not an endorsement or a recommendation signal we have measured.

For Google-specific eligibility, consult its separate [product snippet requirements](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product-snippet) and [merchant listing requirements](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/merchant-listing). These are Google feature rules, not ChatGPT requirements. Google's [structured-data policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies) require representative visible content and do not guarantee a rich result even when markup is correct.

## How does ChatGPT select shopping products?

OpenAI's [shopping documentation](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-with-chatgpt-search) says product selection considers the user's request and context, structured metadata from first- and third-party providers, other third-party content, model responses and product policies. It also notes that not every available product will be shown. That is broader than counting directory mentions or checking for a particular JSON-LD type.

Shopping product results and a conversational recommendation for B2B software are different observations. Do not assume a shopping integration's requirements describe every software-category answer. OpenAI also describes [merchant product feeds](https://chatgpt.com/merchants/); eligible merchants can review that route separately. Adding a feed and adding on-page schema are different tasks. A JSON-LD test does not validate a feed or prove its acceptance.

OpenAI describes shopping product results as separate from ads. Paid advertising is not proof of organic recommendation inclusion. Check current platform documentation before making participation or availability claims.

## A practical product-page audit before measuring recommendations

### 1. Make the buyer's decision possible on the page

State what the product does, who it suits, its limitations, and the facts a buyer needs to compare it. For the lamp, that means light output, adjustment, variant and current offer. For software, that might mean supported integrations, deployment options and a clear explanation of the applicable plan. Link evidence for claims and maintain accurate documentation; do not manufacture reviews or purchase links.

### 2. Compare visible facts, markup and feeds

Check one real product URL first. Compare its name, variant, image, price, currency and stock status across the rendered page, structured data and any feed you maintain. A correct JSON value can still describe the wrong variant. Fix the source of the mismatch rather than adding another conflicting script.

### 3. Validate the intended declaration

Use [Schema Markup Validator](https://validator.schema.org/) for vocabulary checks, and Google's Rich Results Test for supported Google search features. Use AEOCheck's [JSON-LD checker](/tools/schema-checker) to inspect fetched HTML and selected field checklists. Its block number and node path help locate repeated declarations; its checks do not expand contexts or validate every nested field or `@id` reference.

For example, `/@graph/1` identifies the Product node above. If a checker highlights that declaration, compare it with the visible product before editing. A field-check pass is not full semantic validation, feed acceptance or ChatGPT inclusion. The [schema testing guide](/blog/how-to-check-schema-markup) explains the differences between these checks.

### 4. Check access for the intended purpose

OpenAI distinguishes search access through OAI-SearchBot from potential training use through GPTBot in its [crawler documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots). Those controls are independent. Do not enable training just because you want search access.

Test the product URL's rules with the [AI crawler checker](/tools/ai-crawler-checker). Then separately verify redirects, firewall/CDN rules and the content a verified crawler can receive. The tool reads robots.txt; it does not test actual bot access, visits, indexing or citations. An allowed rule is only one piece of evidence.

## Measure readiness, recommendations and business outcomes separately

Use an observation sheet with these columns. These are suggested measurement fields, not results from a customer campaign.

| Evidence layer | What to record | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Page readiness | URL, test date, fetched content, markup/access findings and fixes | That ChatGPT saw or recommended the page |
| Actual answer | Exact prompt, date, product/mode, locale, constraints, mention, recommendation and source URLs | A stable rank or coverage of all possible answers |
| Business outcome | Available consent-respecting referral, scanner or sales events and their dates | That an individual content edit caused the outcome |

Start with unbranded buyer questions such as "Which adjustable desk lamps fit a small desk and provide about 800 lm?" For software, use a genuine category and requirements rather than inserting your brand into every prompt. Keep the prompt set and context consistent, repeat observations, and record omissions as well as mentions. Save citations when present and verify that the linked page supports the claim. A brand mention, a recommendation and a linked citation are separate fields.

Use the existing [ChatGPT measurement guide](/blog/how-to-check-if-website-appears-in-chatgpt-recommendations) for a repeatable protocol. AEOCheck's public scanner checks 25 deterministic readiness signals, with optional generated recommendations. Its score and readiness monitoring do not measure live ChatGPT inclusion or alert you whenever a competitor gains a citation.

There is no reliable universal time-to-recommendation promise. Review changes against comparable observation periods, and keep Google Search Console clicks and impressions separate from AI-answer observations. If referral or conversion data is unavailable, record that gap rather than substituting a score.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What schema markup helps with ChatGPT product recommendations?**

Use Product for a product page, SoftwareApplication for software, and Organization for the business. Add truthful Offer details where applicable. These describe your content; no schema type guarantees a ChatGPT recommendation. FAQPage is for genuine visible questions and answers, not a product-discovery shortcut.

**Can I pay to appear in ChatGPT product recommendations?**

OpenAI describes shopping product results as separate from ads. Buying an ad is not evidence of organic recommendation inclusion. Product feed participation is another data route; check OpenAI's current merchant documentation for access and requirements.

**How do I know if ChatGPT is recommending my product?**

Record actual answers to a consistent set of unbranded product-category questions, including date, mode, locale, constraints, mentions and source URLs. Repeat the observations because answers vary. A website readiness score does not establish that ChatGPT recommended or cited you.

**Does Google ranking affect ChatGPT product recommendations?**

A Google position is not a ChatGPT recommendation measurement. Review Google search performance separately from observed ChatGPT answers; neither proves inclusion in the other.

**How many citations do I need to appear in ChatGPT recommendations?**

There is no documented universal citation-count threshold in the sources reviewed here. Publish accurate product facts and useful evidence, and measure actual answers rather than buying mentions or treating a directory count as eligibility.
