To check whether your website appears in **ChatGPT recommendations**, test the kinds of questions your prospective customers actually ask, then record two separate outcomes: **brand mentions** and **links or citations to your domain**. Repeat the tests over time because answers can vary by wording, context and search availability.

You can also run a [free AEOCheck readiness scan](/) to find technical obstacles, but a readiness score is **not** a live measurement of ChatGPT citations. These are different questions and require different tests.

## Why should you test ChatGPT recommendations?

A buyer might ask ChatGPT for software, agencies or products in a category instead of visiting a conventional search results page first. Seeing your brand in one answer is useful to know, but it is not proof that you will appear for other people, prompts, regions or future searches.

The goal of testing is to establish a reproducible baseline: which prompts mention your business, which cite your website, and which comparable competitors appear. Avoid inferring a market share, ranking position or conversion rate from one generated answer.

## How does ChatGPT search discover sources?

ChatGPT can use web search to answer questions with current information and sometimes include citations. OpenAI says its search ranking uses multiple factors and that placement is not guaranteed. See the official [ChatGPT search guide](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt) and [publisher FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq).

There is a practical difference between a response based mainly on model knowledge and a response that performs a web search. The latter may provide source links that can be checked; the former may mention a brand without citing any current page.

You cannot determine precisely which training examples or ranking factors caused a particular recommendation just by reading an answer. Treat confident explanations of a model's internal reasons with caution.

## Step 1: Build a repeatable prompt set

Choose five to ten prompts that represent genuine buyer intent. For a CRM company, examples might be:

- What CRM tools work well for a small consulting agency?
- Which CRM has a simple setup for a five-person sales team?
- What alternatives should I compare to [a known competitor]?
- Which CRM options include email integration for a startup?
- Is [your brand] a fit for a small team?

Run the prompts in fresh conversations, specify that ChatGPT should search the web when you want current results, and keep the wording consistent between measurement rounds.

Try multiple runs of each prompt because answers can vary. Record the test date, question, whether web search ran, brand mention, domain citation, and relevant competitors. This is a small observational sample, not a scientific estimate of total recommendation share.

## Step 2: Separate mentions from citations

| Outcome | What you can conclude | What you cannot conclude |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand is mentioned, no URL | The answer named your brand in that test | The model fetched or cited your website |
| Your domain appears as a source | That response surfaced a link to your site | Every similar prompt will cite it |
| Competitors appear but you do not | Those competitors appeared in the sampled response | Your site has a particular technical error |
| You appear only when named explicitly | Your brand can be discussed when prompted | You rank for broad category discovery |
| No brand appears | The response didn't recommend a named brand | Your site is blocked from every AI search system |

When a response includes links, open the cited sources and verify they actually support what the answer says. OpenAI cautions that search results and citations can be incomplete or incorrect.

## Step 3: Check whether your public website is accessible

If your domain rarely appears in live-search citations, begin with the fundamentals that you can verify directly.

1. **Search crawler access.** OpenAI identifies OAI-SearchBot as the crawler relevant to ChatGPT search inclusion. GPTBot serves a different training-related role; allowing GPTBot is not the same as allowing OAI-SearchBot. Use the [AI crawler checker](/tools/ai-crawler-checker) to inspect your rules, and consult [OpenAI's publisher FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq).
2. **Noindex and canonical.** Verify you haven't mistakenly blocked indexing or pointed the preferred canonical at an unrelated page.
3. **Readable content.** Make your business name, product category, audience and features visible in the HTML and understandable without relying on a complex interaction.
4. **Accurate metadata.** A descriptive title, useful summary and sensible headings tell visitors what the page offers. Test these with the [meta tag checker](/tools/meta-tag-checker).
5. **Relevant structured data.** If you publish Organization, SoftwareApplication or Product markup, keep it accurate and matched to your visible page. See [how to check schema markup](/blog/how-to-check-schema-markup).

Not every absent citation is a crawlability problem. A webpage may be technically healthy but simply not be the most relevant source for a particular question.

## Step 4: Run a technical readiness audit without confusing it with visibility tracking

AEOCheck checks site-level signals such as crawl access, headings, metadata, structured data, readability and trust information. These checks can help you identify fixable issues, but they do not establish that ChatGPT actually recommended or cited your site.

When comparing audits, distinguish:

- **Readiness:** Are the page's technical and content foundations clear and accessible?
- **Observed mention:** Did a particular AI response name the brand?
- **Observed citation:** Did that response link to the domain or URL?
- **Traffic:** Did a human click through to the website?
- **Conversion:** Did that visitor complete a business goal?

Tracking these separately prevents misleading success claims. A higher readiness score does not automatically imply more mentions, clicks or sales.

## Step 5: Research competing sources without copying their content

For prompts where competitors are cited, examine the cited pages. Ask what task they answer and what evidence they offer: original product documentation, current pricing, specific examples, comparisons, reviews or independent reporting.

Look for gaps your business can genuinely fill. You might create a clear feature page, publish an honest implementation walkthrough, provide a case study with real data, or update an outdated FAQ. Do not fabricate reviews, claims or statistics to imitate a competitor.

Relevant third-party mentions can help people discover a business, but there is no verified formula that makes any particular directory listing or number of reviews guarantee ChatGPT placement.

## Step 6: Monitor results over time

Create a simple table or spreadsheet with columns for date, prompt, search mode, brand mentioned (yes/no), domain cited (yes/no), cited URL, competitors and notes. Use the same prompt set in each measurement round.

If you use analytics software, also look for actual visits referred by AI services. A referral visit is different from a mention in an answer, and not every AI-generated discovery will produce a measurable referral. Compare weeks or months, not one unusually successful run.

## What to do next

Start by testing five representative buyer questions and saving the results. If search citations are rare, check OAI-SearchBot access, canonicals, metadata and page readability. Then improve the page that should answer each question and rerun the same prompts after the changes are discoverable.

There is no guaranteed timeline or placement. The useful outcome is a clearer understanding of **what happened in a specific test** and which independently verifiable site issues you can fix.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I check if ChatGPT recommends my website?

Ask realistic buyer questions, repeat them in fresh sessions, and log both brand mentions and actual domain citations. Use web search for current-source testing and keep the prompt set consistent over time.

### Can a technical AEO scan prove ChatGPT is citing me?

No. A readiness audit checks your page. It does not measure generated responses from ChatGPT or any other answer engine.

### Which OpenAI crawler matters for ChatGPT search?

OAI-SearchBot is relevant to ChatGPT search discovery; GPTBot is separate. An allowed robots rule does not guarantee inclusion.

### How long does it take to appear in ChatGPT search?

There is no fixed timeline. Results depend on crawling, source selection and the particular search query.

### Does schema markup guarantee a recommendation?

No. Schema clarifies structured information, but it does not force an AI model to recommend or cite your business.
